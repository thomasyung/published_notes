# **7 Innovative Tips to Better Your Login Form**

### Keys to a faster sign-in

June 5, 2025

If the sign-up form is the door to your app, the login form is the key. Unlocking the door can be a slow and frustrating process if you don't have the right key or if you've lost your keys. Many users experience this issue on most login forms today because they're not designed with user-friendliness in mind.

When users fail to log in to their accounts, they’ll blame your app. Such an awful experience could lead to them sending you many support emails, creating multiple accounts, or canceling service. If you want users to log in to your app without a hitch, follow these innovative tips most designers aren’t aware of.

## **1) Use "Create Account" with "Sign In"**

The word "login" comes from the verb "log in." It's an outdated technical term that describes how early computers keep a log of users' access to the system. The term "log" originates from the chip log, which was historically used to record the distance traveled at sea ([source](https://substack.com/redirect/7a4cbe08-29f6-468c-8b91-34dd2735860e?j=eyJ1IjoiZTZuNGcifQ.18SDaGq3RvkQtvU8G5iwMTR7KC26K10FdCzravAJA94)).

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F072ec.png)

In other words, "log" is not the most intuitive term to use based on its ambiguous history. Instead, it's better to use "sign in" because it's more familiar to users. Signing in refers to the literal act of signing one's name into a register to record arrival or presence. This practice is often seen in workplaces or hotels.

However, you don't want to use "sign in" with "sign up" because it'll confuse users when they struggle to distinguish between such similar terms. Instead, you should use "create account" with "sign in" because there's a distinction in diction that visually separates them. Additionally, it highlights the user's account, which is the object they interact with when they log in or sign up.

## **2) Indicate Form Type with Toggle**

Sign-up and login forms often look identical at first glance. As a result, users usually make the mistake of signing up on a login form or logging in on a sign-up form. To prevent this mistake, you should indicate the form type with a toggle button. The toggle state serves as a clear visual cue to inform users which form they're on. It also allows them to quickly switch if they're on the wrong form.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7d555.png)

## **3) Accept Multiple Identifiers**

Most login forms only allow users to enter one unique identifier to sign in. It's usually an email or username, but what happens if they forget which identifier they used on your app?

If the user doesn't remember their email address, allow them to sign in with their username or vice versa. This flexibility enables them to successfully sign in even when they experience a memory lapse. You can even allow them to sign in with their phone number because this is also a unique identifier associated with their account.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd90f0.png)

## **4) Indicate Identifier Errors**

When users fail to sign in, most forms do not indicate which field is causing the error. Instead, the error message only tells them that their username or password is incorrect. This practice is intended to confuse hackers so that they can't identify which data is valid, but it also makes it hard to troubleshoot and correct errors.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F548d6.png)

They could enter the wrong identifier but won't ever know it. Instead, they'll assume they entered the wrong password, resulting in numerous failed attempts until they're locked out of their account. The small security measure isn’t worth this big headache.

To prevent the frustration, inform users whether they entered the correct identifier with onFocus validation. As users type their email or username, they should get feedback on whether that data exists in the database. If it doesn't exist, it should tell users it can't be found and prompt them with a link to create an account.

## **5) Show Reset Links with Errors**

Most login forms display a "Forgot Password?" link below the field for resetting the password. This link can distract and confuse users as they're signing in. It makes more sense to display it after a failed sign-in attempt to avoid cluttering the form. The error message for the password field should provide them with the reset link. Presenting it in context when it’s relevant leads to greater visibility and interaction.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8e36c.png)

## **6) Offer Multiple Sign-In Options**

Signing in with an email and password isn't always what users prefer. Sometimes, they don't even want to bother with typing and remembering passwords because it requires too high of a cognitive load.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe08b8.png)

You can make their lives easier by offering multiple sign-in options that don't require a password. Signing in with a phone number allows users to enter a code they receive via text message. Signing in with a link sends them an email where they can sign in by clicking the link.

Additionally, you can offer third-party sign-in options such as Google and Apple. Since most users already have an account with these services, they don't need to create another account and password to access your app.

## **7) Indicate the Last Sign-In Option**

The typical user has multiple accounts across the internet. They won't always sign in with the same method on every app. As a result, they might forget which sign-in option they last used on your app and choose the wrong one.

To help them remember, you can put a "Last sign-in" badge next to the sign-in option they chose. You can achieve this by storing the user's login information in a keychain that's synced across devices. Each device that signs in to an account has a unique device ID that tracks login activity. An app that does this with their login is TikTok.
