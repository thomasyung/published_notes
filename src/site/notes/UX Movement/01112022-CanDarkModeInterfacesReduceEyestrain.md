# **01/11/2022 - **[**Can Dark Mode Interfaces Reduce Eyestrain?**](https://uxmovement.substack.com/p/can-dark-mode-interfaces-reduce-eyestrain)

### The truth about dark color schemes

Nearly 70% of Americans suffer from digital eyestrain ([source](https://www.reviewob.com/how-many-americans-experience-digital-eye-strain/)). The average American spends over 7 hours per day looking at a screen ([source](https://www.comparitech.com/tv-streaming/screen-time-statistics/)). It’s no wonder why eyestrain is such a common problem.

Cutting back screen time can reduce eyestrain, but there might be a way designers and developers can help mitigate this problem. Dark color schemes for interfaces could improve the user’s viewing experience.

## **Low Ambient Light**

Most interfaces default to a light mode that displays dark text on a light background. While light mode is beneficial for text contrast and legibility, the brightness of the light background can strain the eyes under certain conditions.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fbucketeer-e05bbc84-baa3-437e-9518-adb32be77984.s3.amazonaw_585.png)

If you’re in a place with low ambient light, a light background will cause the pupils to work harder to adjust to rapid light changes. The pupils will dilate when there’s less light and constrict when there’s more light. This constant dilation and constriction tire the eye muscles, leading to eyestrain.

The degree of dilation is determined by the amount of light hitting the entire visual field, not just a single point or area. That means any darkness surrounding a light background will bleed into the visual field and affect the pupil. Focusing on the screen alone without looking anywhere else would not prevent eyestrain.

Research shows that reducing the contrast between the perceived brightness of a screen and ambient light reduces eyestrain ([source](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2006/04/060425015643.htm)). A dark mode background would cause the pupils to constrict less and stay dilated. As a result, users can focus better without experiencing visual fatigue.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fbucketeer-e05bbc84-baa3-437e-9518-adb32be77984.s3.amazonaw_586.png)

## **High Ambient Light**

Dark mode in high ambient light causes eyestrain. The high contrast between the screen and surrounding light creates tension in the eye muscles. They're fighting between constriction and dilation, which overworks the pupils.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fbucketeer-e05bbc84-baa3-437e-9518-adb32be77984.s3.amazonaw_587.png)

However, a light mode in high ambient light does not cause eyestrain. The pupil stays constricted because the contrast level of the screen and the light around it matches.

Since ambient light levels change throughout the day, it's crucial to provide a light and dark mode that users can switch. Allowing them to switch to the appropriate color scheme gives them control to choose what's best for their eyes.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fbucketeer-e05bbc84-baa3-437e-9518-adb32be77984.s3.amazonaw_588.png)

## **How Dark Mode Affects Sleep**

Nearly 75% of Americans use smartphones or computers before bed ([source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3836340/)). A dark mode would allow many nighttime users to view interfaces without disrupting their circadian rhythms and get better sleep.

One study found that exposure to different light intensities before bedtime alters circadian timing ([source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4053688/)). Participants exposed to higher intensity light had later circadian timing by about one hour.

Circadian misalignment negatively impacts alertness and performance. Dark mode may be the answer to prevent this because it exposes users to lower intensity light.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fbucketeer-e05bbc84-baa3-437e-9518-adb32be77984.s3.amazonaw_589.png)

Another study found a link between sleep and light exposure at night. Participants exposed to higher ambient light levels during sleep experienced increased eye fatigue the following morning ([source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6137029/)).

What this means is that users are susceptible to light at night. Lower light exposure is better for sleep and ocular health. You don’t have control over the user's ambient light level, but you can control the perceived brightness on the interface with a dark mode.

## **Why Many Are Switching to Dark Mode**

One survey found that 83% of participants use dark mode on their operating system. When asked why, the majority stated the reason is “it’s easier on the eyes” ([source](https://www.designial.com/does-dark-mode-improve-the-user-experience/)).

Another study found that dark mode can significantly increase user engagement ([source](https://web.dev/terra-dark-mode/)). User engagement nearly doubled for mobile and tripled for desktop.

![](/img/user/attachments/https%3A%2F%2Fbucketeer-e05bbc84-baa3-437e-9518-adb32be77984.s3.amazonaw_590.png)

The results make sense because if users experience eyestrain, the time they spend viewing an interface would decrease. If they don’t experience eyestrain, they’re more willing to view more pages per visit.

## **Should You Design a Dark Mode?**

Since ambient light affects eyestrain, a dark mode might not make sense if most of your users only view your interface in the daytime. Check your analytics to see what time of day your users are most active.

In the future, more and more users will demand dark mode as they experience eyestrain. Instead of being an option, dark mode will become a necessity similar to accessibility on apps and websites. Start making your interfaces night-friendly, and you'll provide a more comfortable viewing experience for the many who suffer from digital eyestrain.
